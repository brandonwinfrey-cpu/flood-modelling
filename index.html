Climate action · Informal settlements

Same street, same storm, more water

Climate change is making heavy storms stronger. We use drones, terrain maps and flood models to show how much deeper and faster floodwater could get on a walkway in an informal settlement, and what that means for the people who live there.

What you saw at the display

One 100-year flood, today and in 2100

A “100-year flood” is a storm with a 1 in 100 chance of happening in any year. Here is how it looks on a walkway at [site name].

Today · 0.2 m deep
Calf deep

Walkable but slippery. Floodwater can carry sewage and rubbish.

2100 · 0.4 m deep
Knee deep

Twice as deep, and moving faster. Harder to stand in and unsafe for children and older people.

Depths are modelled estimates, using terrain data from drone surveys. The scenes above are illustrations, not model output.

How we do it

From drone to risk

Four steps turn a 20-minute flight into a picture of flood risk that communities and decision-makers can use.

Fly

A drone photographs the settlement from above.

Map

The photos become a detailed 3D terrain map, including small drains, steps and low spots that steer the water.

Model

A computer model runs a design storm over that terrain to show where the water goes and how deep it gets, today and in a warmer climate.

Assess risk

Depth and flow are translated into what they mean for people: slippery, hard to walk, unsafe.

About

Who is doing this work

This work is carried out by the Revitalising Informal Settlements and their Environments (RISE) program and the Department of Civil and Environmental Engineering, Monash University.

RISE is a transdisciplinary research program on health, environment, and water and sanitation. It works with communities, governments and local partners to co-design water-sensitive solutions for informal settlements. Learn more at rise-program.org.
